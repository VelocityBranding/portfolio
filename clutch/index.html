Weekly testing rhythm

How I'd run creative testing at Clutch

The rules I use today, mapped onto Meta lead gen. Every concept gets a verdict within the week.

Mon

Brief

3–5 new concepts per account from client info, CRM notes and last week's winners. One angle per concept, three hooks each.

Tue–Wed

Launch

Editor builds; concepts go live in a testing campaign with equal budget, so each one gets a fair read.

Thu

Read

Hook rate, hold rate, CTR and cost per lead in Ads Manager, then lead quality and bookings in the CRM.

Fri

Decide

Losers off. Winners move to scaling with 3 new variations. One-line summary to the client.

Spec work · written for this application

Sample concepts for your kind of clients

Three briefs the way I'd hand them to your editor. Offers and prices are placeholders; I'd never invent a client's offer.

Gym · lead gen

"Start before January"

Angle: people who plan to start in January can beat the rush and the crowds.

"Everyone's joining in January. Here's why our best members started in November."

  1. 0–3s: busy January gym footage, text on screen: "January: queue for every machine"
  2. 3–10s: the same gym on a quiet November morning, a coach greeting a new member by name
  3. 10–20s: member, to camera: why starting now made it stick
  4. 20–25s: [offer] for November starters, "tap to claim your spot"

Editor: 9:16 and 4:5 · 25s · captions burned in · cut on every line · hook text from frame 0 · no logo intro.

Judge on: cost per lead, then trial-to-member rate in the CRM.

Physio clinic · lead gen

"The desk test"

Angle: a 30-second self-test that shows the viewer they have the problem before we offer the fix.

"Sit at your desk and try this. If it hurts, keep watching."

  1. 0–3s: physio at a desk demonstrating a simple neck rotation
  2. 3–15s: "If you felt a pull here, it's usually this", shown on a model
  3. 15–22s: one quick relief stretch the viewer can do now (value first)
  4. 22–30s: "Book a [offer] assessment, and we'll find the cause"

Editor: real clinician, not an actor · natural light · arrows and text callouts on the body · 30s cut plus a 15s cutdown.

Judge on: booked assessments, not form fills.

Beauty · lead gen

"What actually happens"

Angle: first-timers fear the unknown. Show the appointment from the client's seat.

"Nervous about your first skin consultation? Here's every minute of it."

  1. 0–3s: POV walking in, text: "my first consultation"
  2. 3–15s: the consultation, quick cuts: questions, skin check, plan explained
  3. 15–22s: client, to camera: "I thought it'd be pushy. It wasn't."
  4. 22–28s: "[offer] consultations this month, book in 30 seconds"

Editor: phone-shot look, not polished · no prescription-medicine names in the ad (UK rules) · results claims only if the client can prove them.

Judge on: consultation show rate and bookings.

Beyond the ads

The 6 checks I'd run on a new account

Most "bad ads" are a problem somewhere else in the funnel. This is my first-week order.

1

Tracking

Pixel and Conversions API firing on the right event, leads matching between Ads Manager and the CRM.

2

Funnel maths

CPM → CTR → cost per click → conversion rate → cost per lead → booked → showed → sold. Find the step that breaks.

3

Creative fatigue

Frequency rising, hook rate falling, the same winner running for weeks. Time for new concepts.

4

Structure

Budget spread too thin to leave learning, or audiences overlapping. Simplify.

5

Offer and page

Is the offer clear in 3 seconds? Does the landing page or form load fast on a phone and ask only what's needed?

6

Speed to lead

How fast does someone call back? From recruitment: the first reply usually wins.
